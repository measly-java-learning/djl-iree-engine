There's an unexplored alternative and an unexplored win. The staged input copy running 3–35× a bare memcpy suggests IREE allocates a fresh staging buffer per call — caching that might recover much of the benefit with none of the alignment or lifetime contract, and it's worth a paragraph before committing to borrowing. Separately, the output path's two copies (§2) exist because "nothing IREE-side may outlive Invoke" — a constraint that synchronous-only execution now satisfies trivially. Mapping directly into the destination buffer and skipping the intermediate std::vector halves output copies with no contract change at all, and output cost tracks input cost in your table.

Both items prototyped and measured 2026-08-04: cached staging recovers ~85% of the staged-vs-wrapped delta (JNI default flipped to kCachedMapWrite), and direct output mapping halves the output path. Findings: docs/2026-08-04-staging-and-output-findings.md.
